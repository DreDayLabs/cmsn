# CMSN — What AI (Claude & Agents) Can and Cannot Do for This Build

You asked directly: what can these models do, what can't they do, and where do they fit in design, tech packs, sourcing, and growth. Here's the honest map. The pattern that repeats: **AI compresses the thinking, drafting, and exploring; humans (you, a technical designer, a factory) own the physical and the final.**

## Design & identity

**Claude/AI can:**
- Generate and iterate logo/wordmark concepts, moodboards, and lookbook imagery (Firefly, Midjourney, and the image tools connected to this workspace).
- Write the design brief that gets a human designer to the right answer in one round instead of four ([02](02-identity-and-logo.md) *is* that brief).
- Produce print-ready graphic layouts for tees (the formula diagrams are exactly the kind of vector-diagram work AI + Illustrator does well — draft the diagram, you refine in Illustrator).
- Critique: drop a candidate logo/mockup in chat and get a structured read against the brief (scale test, black-garment test, comparisons to competitor marks).
- Drive your Adobe stack: this workspace has Adobe tools connected (asset workflows, image edits, background removal, vectorize) — batch mockup prep and photo cleanup are delegable.

**AI cannot:**
- Deliver final ownable letterforms. AI output is raster-ish, near-derivative of existing fonts, and legally murky as trademark material — the type designer step in [02](02-identity-and-logo.md) is not skippable.
- Judge physical craft: how an embroidery digitizes, how a puff print cracks, how white ink sits on black poly. Strike-offs (physical print tests) are the only truth.

## Tech packs & product development

**Claude/AI can:**
- Draft the full tech-pack document: cover sheet, flat-sketch callouts, bill of materials (fabric, GSM, trims, zippers, thread), points of measure with a graded size table, stitch/seam specs, label placement, packaging spec. This is structured-document work — squarely in the wheelhouse.
- Build the **big-and-tall grading tables** — extending a size chart to 2XL–4XL with longer inseams/torsos and recalculated points of measure is spreadsheet math AI does fast (and it's your product moat, see [04](04-product-and-manufacturing.md)).
- Translate: turn your plain-English intent ("track jacket that hits below the hip, heavier zipper, no drawcord") into factory-standard spec language, and translate factory replies back.
- Generate flat sketches (technical line drawings) as starting points via image tools — usable for quoting, though factories will want cleaner vectors for production.

**AI cannot:**
- **Sign off a spec.** A tech pack drafted by AI and unreviewed by a human technical designer will contain fit errors you'll discover in $150 samples and 6-week round trips. Budget one freelance technical-designer review pass (~$300–800 per style on Upwork/apparel-specific marketplaces) before anything goes to a factory. AI drafts, human verifies, factory executes.
- Feel fabric. GSM and fiber specs on paper ≠ hand-feel. Order swatch cards. Always.
- Fit a body. You are the fit model for the men's line — that's an advantage, use it every sample round.

## Sourcing & manufacturing

**Claude/AI can:**
- Research and shortlist factories (the deep-research sweep feeding [04](04-product-and-manufacturing.md) is exactly this), compare MOQs/prices, and flag red flags in supplier profiles.
- Draft every factory communication: RFQs, sample requests, negotiation replies, QC checklists, and the polite-but-firm email when a sample comes back wrong.
- Model the money: landed-cost calculators, margin scenarios, cash-flow timing of deposit/balance payments.

**AI cannot:**
- Verify a factory is real and good. That takes sample orders, video calls, and (at production scale) a third-party inspection (~$300/day via QIMA/V-Trust-type services).
- Negotiate relationships. Templates help; the relationship is yours.

## Content, Instagram & growth

**Claude/AI can:**
- Build and maintain the 90-day calendar in [05](05-content-and-community.md); write hooks, captions, scripts, and carousels in the brand voice; batch 30 caption variants and A/B them.
- Edit-adjacent work via connected tools: clip long footage into shorts, resize/reframe for platforms, enhance audio, generate concept imagery for The Word campaign posts.
- Analyze: paste your metrics and get format-level readouts (what to double down on) plus weekly content retros.
- Run the machine around commerce: Shopify product copy, email/SMS flows (welcome, waitlist, drop, post-purchase), customer-service macros.

**AI cannot:**
- Be you. The engine in [05](05-content-and-community.md) has one non-substitutable input: your face, your body, your walk, 4–5×/week. AI-generated "founder" content in a founder-led brand is detectable and fatal to trust.
- Manufacture community. It can draft replies; the DMs that build the first 1,000 true fans need to actually be from The CMSNer.

## Legal & ops

**Can:** run trademark knockout pre-searches and summarize the neighborhood; draft the attorney briefing memo; draft operating-agreement and IP-assignment *drafts* for attorney review; keep the compliance checklist ([03](03-legal-and-entity.md)).
**Cannot:** be your attorney, file with legal standing on your behalf, or issue a registrability opinion you should rely on.

## The division of labor, one line each

- **AI:** research, drafts, math, mockups, calendars, copy, critique, translation, iteration speed.
- **You:** taste, face, fit model, relationships, final calls, consistency.
- **Paid humans (small, targeted):** type designer ($1.5–5k, once), technical-designer review (~$300–800/style, per capsule), trademark attorney ($1.2–2.4k, once), third-party QC (~$300/production run).

That's the whole staffing plan for year one.
