# CMSN — Legal, Entity & Trademark

> Not legal advice — this is the map so your 30–60 minutes with an actual trademark attorney is cheap and productive instead of exploratory and expensive.

## Entity: new LLC vs parking it under an existing company

**Recommendation: form a new, dedicated LLC for CMSN.** You mentioned owning several LLCs and Incs it could live under — here's the calculus:

**Why a dedicated LLC wins:**
- **Clean IP ownership.** The trademark, the custom letterforms, the domain, the Shopify store, factory contracts — all owned by one entity whose only business is CMSN. If the brand works, this is what makes it *sellable or investable* without surgery.
- **Liability isolation.** Apparel carries product-liability exposure (compression wear especially — someone will claim a seam failure or skin reaction eventually). You don't want that reaching your other businesses, and you don't want your other businesses' liabilities reaching CMSN.
- **Clean books from day one.** Sampling, inventory, ad spend, and revenue in one P&L makes the "is this working?" question answerable — and makes the eventual accountant/investor conversation trivial.

**The counter-case (using an existing entity):** faster start (skip formation), one less annual filing/fee, and if an existing Inc already has apparel-adjacent activity or an S-corp election you like, there's a tax-simplicity argument. If you go this route, at minimum register **"CMSN" as a DBA** of that entity and keep a separate bank account and bookkeeping class for the brand. But given you already run multiple entities, you know the overhead is small — take the clean structure.

**Mechanics:**
1. File **CMSN LLC** (fallbacks: CMSN Apparel LLC, CMSN Athletics LLC) in your home state. NY filing is ~$200 plus the publication requirement (~$300–1,200 depending on county — Bronx/NYC counties are the expensive ones; some founders form in a cheaper state, but for a single-owner operating business, home state is usually simplest and avoids foreign-qualification duplication).
2. Get the **EIN** at irs.gov — free, online, ~10 minutes, immediately issued. You need the LLC first.
3. Open a **business bank account** (Mercury/Relay are the fast online options; a local bank works too). Every CMSN dollar flows through it from day zero.
4. Assign existing assets **into** the LLC: the earnyourcmsn.com domain, the Shopify store, the Instagram/TikTok handles, and (when delivered) the logo IP assignment from your type designer should all name the LLC, not you personally.

## Trademark strategy

### The good news about the name change

**CMSN is a dramatically stronger trademark than the dictionary word it's short for.** That spelled-out word is common with a crowded register — a Class 25 application for it alone would face serious 2(d) (likelihood-of-confusion) risk and enforcement would be weak. **CMSN is a coined four-letter mark** — inherently distinctive, easier to register, easier to enforce, and it matches your domain and handle. This is the same structural move as ASOS, GOAT, or SKIMS: compressed, ownable, brandable.

### What to file, in order

1. **Word mark: "CMSN"** — International Class 25 (clothing: shirts, pants, leggings, shorts, jackets, compression garments). This is the crown jewel: a *standard character* mark protects the name in any font, so it isn't hostage to logo redesigns. Consider adding **Class 18** (gym bags) and **Class 21** (water bottles) later — you literally described the walk with the gym bag and bottle; those are natural extensions, but each class costs money, so start with 25.
2. **Word mark: "EARN YOUR CMSN"** — Class 25, matching the domain and the locked naming rule (the brand name is never spelled out). Slogans are registrable when used as a brand (on hang tags, neck labels, marketing) and not merely ornamentally — which means: put it on labels and tags, not only splashed across shirt backs. A tagline used *only* as a big decorative back print can be refused as "ornamental use"; the neck-label use is what makes it function as a mark.
3. **(Optional, later) Stylized mark** — the final designed CMSN wordmark, once the type designer delivers. Only worth it if the letterforms are genuinely distinctive (direction 3 in [02-identity-and-logo.md](02-identity-and-logo.md)).

### Before filing: the knockout search (do this now, it's free)

- Search **USPTO** (tmsearch.uspto.gov) for: `CMSN` and `C.M.S.N.` in Class 25/18/21, plus lookalike neighbors (`CMS`, `CSN` in apparel). Because trademark conflicts include *phonetic* equivalents, you must also search the spoken word literally — `COMMISSION` and `EARN YOUR COMMISSION` in Class 25 — to map the neighborhood a CMSN application will be compared against. (These literal strings are search terms only; per the naming rule they never appear in brand copy.)
- Search Google, Instagram, TikTok, and Amazon for "CMSN" apparel/fitness uses — **common-law rights matter in the US** even without registration.
- Claude can run and summarize this sweep for you (see [06-ai-capabilities-map.md](06-ai-capabilities-map.md)); a paid attorney "full search + opinion" (~$500–1,500) is worth it before you spend real money on inventory, not necessarily before filing.

### Filing mechanics & cost

- **DIY via USPTO TEAS:** $350 per class per mark (base fee, 2025 fee schedule; surcharges apply for custom identification wording — using pre-approved ID Manual descriptions keeps it at base). So CMSN + tagline in Class 25 ≈ **$700 DIY**.
- **With a trademark attorney:** typically $600–1,200 per mark all-in. Given the brand's importance and that office actions are common for first-timers, the attorney route is the recommended spend — this is a "buy it once" item.
- **Intent-to-use (1(b)) filing:** you can and should file **before** products ship — an ITU application locks your priority date now; you file the "statement of use" (with photos of real products/labels) once you're selling ($100/class more). **Sequencing: finalize the wordmark → knockout search → file ITU → launch → statement of use.**
- Timeline reality: ~8–14 months to registration. The ® waits; the priority date doesn't. File within the next 60 days.

## The compliance small print (cheap now, expensive later)

- **Labeling:** US apparel requires fiber content, country of origin, care instructions, and RN number (or your company name) on labels — your factory handles printing, but *you* supply the copy; the FTC Textile Rules are the reference. Get an RN number free from the FTC once the LLC exists.
- **Formula/peptide tees:** the "how to bench" diagram tees are pure content — no issue. For anything referencing peptides/supplement stacks, keep it clearly satirical/cultural and make no health claims on garments or captions; "I'm on peptides" as a joke tee is culture, a dosing protocol is FDA/FTC bait. When in doubt, the joke version ships and the protocol version doesn't.
- **Sales tax:** Shopify + a tax app handles nexus; just don't ignore it after the first few hundred orders.
