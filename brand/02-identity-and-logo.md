## The mark system

You don't need one logo. You need a small system, because apparel branding lives at wildly different scales — a 1cm chest hit and a 40cm jacket back are different jobs.

| Mark | What it is | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary wordmark: CMSN** | Four letters, engineered sans, condensed, generous tracking | Chest hit (left chest or center-chest under the neck), waistbands, leg hits on leggings/compression, zipper pulls, labels, app icon, IG avatar |
| **Statement lockup: oversized CMSN** | The same four letters scaled up — big bold white letters, shoulder-to-shoulder | Jacket backs, the long knee-length track jacket, hoodie-alternative pieces — the "everyone in the elevator reads it" piece |
| **Tagline lockup: EARN YOUR CMSN** | Three words, stacked or single-line | Inside neck print, hang tags, campaign graphics, the back of formula tees, packaging |
| **Standalone symbol: //** | Two source-derived slashes; highest-recognition asset | Garments, bags, bottles, labels, embroidery and digital icons |

Small CMSN is quiet; large CMSN or COMMISSION is a statement. The standalone // symbol leads recognition across garments and accessories.

## The typography brief (hand this to any designer or AI)

> Production direction: retain the approved CMSN letter masters and standalone slash source. Build outlined black/white CMSN, COMMISSION and CMSN// variants from those assets. The old exploration brief and spelling prohibition are superseded by founder direction dated 2026-09-14.

### Three concept directions to explore first

1. **"Machined"** — near-monoline geometric sans (think DIN/Neue Haas Grotesk DNA), squared counters, the M and N given flat apexes so the four letters read as one engineered unit. Closest to: Nike ACG, On Running's type. Feels: technical, quiet, expensive.
2. **"Compressed authority"** — heavy condensed grotesque, tight but never touching, slightly extended tracking at small sizes. The jacket-back CMSN in this cut is a wall of type. Closest to: Fear of God Essentials' back prints, Represent 247. Feels: statement, streetwear-literate, Bronx.
3. **"Cut letters"** — start from a clean grotesque, then make 2–3 surgical customizations: notch the C so it echoes an "open rep" / unfinished circle, unify the diagonals of M and N, square the S. This is the direction most likely to yield an ownable mark and a natural monogram (that notched C). Feels: designed, not typed.

Generate all three, put them on mockups (see workflow below), and let the mockups decide — a logo that looks great on a white artboard and dead on a black track jacket loses.

## AI vs Fiverr vs designer vs custom font — the honest decision

| Path | Cost | What you get | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pure AI (Firefly/Midjourney/Claude + your Adobe suite)** | ~$0 | Fast, unlimited exploration; raster concepts you must rebuild as vectors; letterforms will be *near* existing fonts | **Use for exploration, not the final mark.** It's how you figure out what you like without spending money. |
| **Fiverr ($50–300 tier)** | Cheap | Usually a lightly-tracked existing font + a generic icon. High plagiarism/reuse risk — a real problem when you later try to trademark or enforce | **Skip for the core mark.** Fine later for one-off graphic tees. |
| **Independent brand/type designer ($1.5k–5k)** | Mid | Custom-drawn or heavily-customized letterforms, vector masters, usage guide, and IP assignment in the contract — letterforms you actually own | **This is the move.** Find them via Behance/Dribbble/Instagram searching "wordmark" + "athletic" — or better, DM the designer credited on a brand whose type you admire. |
| **Full custom typeface ($10k–50k+)** | High | A whole font family you own (what Nike, Netflix did) | **Year 2+.** When there's revenue and the brand voice is proven, get a display face designed for campaigns. Not now. |

**The hybrid workflow (recommended, start this week):**

1. **Explore with AI + Adobe (days 1–7).** Generate against the three directions above. In Illustrator, also set "CMSN" in 15–20 licensed fonts you already have via Adobe Fonts (try: Neue Haas Grotesk, Helvetica Now Condensed, DIN 2014, Founders-style grotesques, Druk-alikes) and manually adjust tracking/weights. You're building a taste board, not a final.
2. **Mock everything (days 7–10).** Drop candidates into your mockup packs — black track jacket back, left-chest tee hit, legging waistband, embroidered cap. Kill anything that doesn't survive the 8mm embroidery test and the black-garment test.
3. **Hire the type designer (weeks 2–6, $1.5–5k).** Brief = this document + your top 2 directions + the mockups. Deliverables to require in writing: vector masters (AI/EPS/SVG), black/white versions, the oversized back-print CMSN and the EARN YOUR CMSN tagline lockup in matching letterforms, minimum-size and clear-space rules, **and full IP assignment of the custom letterforms to your LLC.**
4. **Then trademark the final mark** (see [03-legal-and-entity.md](03-legal-and-entity.md)) — file after the wordmark is final, not before.

### Why you can't just use a font and own it

Font licenses (Adobe Fonts included) let you *use* a typeface in a logo, but you don't own the letterform designs — and a logo that is nothing but an unmodified font setting is weak IP: hard to enforce, easy for a competitor to approximate. Custom-drawn or meaningfully modified letterforms are copyrightable design work that gets assigned to you, and they make the stylized trademark stronger. This is exactly why direction 3 ("Cut letters") is the smart target: a few surgical, distinctive modifications convert "typed" into "owned."

## Color, applied

- **Core:** true black `#000000`, off-black `#0A0A0A` (garment-dye reality — pure black fabric is rare), white `#FFFFFF`, navy `#0B1526`-range, one gray `#8A8A8A`-range.
- **Discipline:** logos appear in black or white only. Never gray-on-gray for the mark. Navy garments take the white mark. No outlines, no gradients, no colorways of the logo — colorways are for fabric, not for the mark. This restraint is what reads as expensive.

## What "done" looks like

A one-page brand sheet (make it in Illustrator/InDesign) with: the CMSN wordmark, the oversized statement version, the tagline lockup, minimum sizes, clear space, the five colors, and three photographed/mocked applications. That sheet is what you send factories for strike-offs, embroiderers for digitizing, and any future freelancer. Until that sheet exists, the identity isn't done — a logo file alone is not an identity.
