# CMSN — Wordmark Font Identification & Licensing Research

> Research report (agent-compiled, sourced). Context: the CMSN wordmark drafts were set in an unidentified typeface; the production logo shipped on the site is hand-drawn vector paths, not typed text. Not legal advice — this is the brief for the trademark attorney.

## Likely candidate fonts for the original drafts

The letterform profile — uppercase-only geometric monoline, rounded terminals, very wide tracking, splayed M with pointed apexes and a high center vertex, soft rounded S, rounded-square open C — maps to a known cluster of free futuristic display faces:

| # | Font | Designer | License | Where | Fit notes |
|---|------|----------|---------|-------|-----------|
| 1 | **Anurati** | Emmeran Richard | **FREE FOR PERSONAL USE ONLY** — commercial requires paid "Anurati Pro" | Behance freebie (700k+ downloads) | Top candidate if strokes have gaps/interruptions. Highest-risk license. |
| 2 | **Aquire** | Gregory "Sesohq" Ortiz | **FREE FOR PERSONAL USE ONLY** — commercial by contacting designer | Behance/FontSpace/DaFont | Very popular for logos; same red-flag license. |
| 3 | **Potra** | Alejo Bergmann | Free personal + commercial (reported OFL — verify bundled license text) | Font Squirrel, FontSpace | Rounded-line futuristic, pitched for logotypes. Low risk if OFL confirmed. |
| 4 | **Elianto** | Emanuele Papale | Designer states free personal + commercial (mirrors conflict — trust the Behance release) | Behance/Befonts | Thin geometric monoline. |
| 5 | **Nasalization** | Ray Larabie / Typodermic | Free regular weight incl. commercial/logo use; family paid | Typodermic, Adobe Fonts | The NASA-worm homage; M rounder than the draft's. |

Screened weaker matches: Audiowide (OFL — squared M), Josefin Sans (OFL — matches the splayed M but bookish), Moonhouse (license varies by mirror), Aldrich, Comfortaa, Zekton, Nulshock.

**How to confirm:** run the draft through WhatTheFont/Identifont. Three tells: gapped strokes → Anurati/Aquire; fully rounded no-gap terminals → Potra/Nasalization; pointed M but elegant overall → Josefin Sans.

## The law, in five lines

1. **Typeface designs are NOT copyrightable in the US** (37 CFR §202.1(e); *Eltra v. Ringer*, 4th Cir. 1978). Anyone may imitate letter shapes by eye.
2. **Font FILES are copyrighted software** (Copyright Office 1992 policy; *Adobe v. Southern Software*, N.D. Cal. 1998 — the outline data itself is the protected expression).
3. **EULAs are contracts** that govern use of the file: "free for personal use" excludes any commercial use (apparel is the paradigm case); some commercial EULAs restrict logo use or require an addendum (the Rite Aid and Shake Shack logo-font disputes were exactly this).
4. **Outlining text does not launder a license problem** — a license was needed the moment the text was set, and outline conversion copies the protected outline data.
5. **Trademark is unaffected**: the USPTO doesn't care what font a wordmark uses; OFL/Google Fonts explicitly allow logo use and trademarking.

## Where CMSN stands

- **The production mark (site, brand SVGs, mockups) is hand-drawn vector paths built by eye — no font file was typed, outlined, or traced.** Under the rules above, that puts the shipped mark on solid ground even if the draft font turns out to be personal-use-only: the restriction lives on the software, not the shapes. It is fully trademarkable, and it already carries deliberate modifications (respaced tracking, adjusted proportions).
- **Residual risks to close out, in order:**
  1. **Provenance of the original draft vectors.** If the drafts were made by typing in the font and hitting Create Outlines, the EULA applied at that step. Get a one-line written statement from the designer on how the drafts were made and keep it with brand records.
  2. **If Anurati/Aquire-class font was anywhere in the chain**, buy the commercial license anyway — it's cheap belt-and-suspenders relative to the brand's value.
  3. **Recognizability**: an unmodified famous freebie is a distinctiveness problem more than a legal one — thousands of brands use Anurati. The hand-drawn modifications (and the future type-designer pass) are the answer.
- **Do:** archive the identified font's license text + designer provenance note alongside the trademark filing records. Never use the font's name in marketing or filings.

## Action items

1. Run the original draft through WhatTheFont → identify the source font definitively.
2. Ask the designer, in writing: "Which font were the drafts set in, and were the final vectors typed/outlined or drawn?"
3. If a personal-use-only font touched a commercial workflow → buy its commercial license once.
4. File the license text + provenance note in brand records before the trademark filing.
