# CMSN — Content & Community Engine

You worked Nike marketing for five years — you already know the theory. This file is the operating system: formats, calendar, and mechanics, tuned to what you actually have (you, a phone, an incredible gym one building away, and a story).

Corny is a feature. "The CMSNer" is exactly the kind of self-aware handle that reads as confidence. Under Armour's "We must protect this house" was corny too — corny plus conviction is how slogans get chanted.

## The three content pillars

### 1. The Walk (signature format — own it)
The insight the whole brand is built on: *everyone knows when someone's going to the gym.* So film it, ritually.

- **Format:** 15–30s POV/tracking shots — apartment door, elevator, lobby, the outdoor walk to the next building, gym doors. Gym bag, water bottle, fit on display. Same route, different fits, different weather, different moods. Rain-gear episodes when it rains.
- **Hook lines:** "POV: the commute is 90 seconds and it's still a runway." / "Everyone in this elevator knows where I'm going." / "The gym fit is for the walk, not the workout."
- **Why it works:** it's serialized (people return for the ritual), it's replicable by the community later (#TheWalk in CMSN), and every frame is a product shot that doesn't feel like one.

### 2. The Work (credibility)
Real training. You at 6'4"/250 moving real weight in muted black/white/navy. Occasional formula-tee integration: the "how to bench" tee filmed *while benching* — the shirt is the tutorial, the video is the proof. This pillar is why the brand isn't a costume.

### 3. The Word (community ethic)
"Earn Your CMSN" as content: talking-head or text-on-screen pieces about earning it — in the gym, at work, from the Bronx to the corporate floor. The Equinox lunchtime lifter taking the suit off. The '90s-nostalgia energy you described (era of the big billboard, "We must protect this house") lives here: campaign-grade statements, black screen, white type, no music or one hard track.

## The formula tees as a content engine

The tee line doubles as an infinite content format:
- **Front:** muted — small CMSN at the chest. **Back:** the formula — bench-press setup cues, leg-press foot positions, Bulgarian split-squat diagram, progressive-overload math.
- Each new tee = a launch video (the lift, taught by the shirt) + a carousel (the diagram) + a duet/stitch magnet (people filming their attempt wearing it).
- Community mechanic: **"submit the next formula"** — followers pitch the next tee's diagram; winner gets the drop free and a tag on the back label. That's UGC, product R&D, and community status in one loop.
- Keep the peptide-stack tees in satire register (see legal note in [03](03-legal-and-entity.md)) — "I'm on peptides" the joke shirt, never dosing guidance.

## The 90-day pre-launch calendar (audience before product)

**Cadence: 4–5 posts/week on TikTok + IG Reels (same asset), 2–3 stories/day, 1 carousel/week.** Batch-film weekly — one gym session + one Walk session yields a week of content.

- **Days 1–30 — The person.** No product talk. The Walk (2×/wk), The Work (2×/wk), The Word (1×/wk). Bio says "CMSN — coming January." Goal: find the 2–3 formats that pop; feed the winners. Start the email waitlist link day one — the site already captures email; every video's CTA rotates to it.
- **Days 31–60 — The brand appears.** Behind-the-scenes becomes content: logo directions on the table ("help me pick — 1 or 2?"), fabric swatches arriving, first strike-offs, sample unboxings, sample *failures* (the honest ones outperform). Polls and comment-votes on real decisions — people who vote on the waistband buy the waistband.
- **Days 61–90 — The countdown.** Samples on your body, on the route. The Walk, but now every fit is CMSN. Waitlist push hardens: "first drop is [N] units. Waitlist gets 24 hours before anyone else." Seed 10–20 sample pieces to big guys with mid-size engaged followings (5k–50k) — gifting, no paid posts yet; big men are cheap to seed because nobody sends them anything that fits.

**KPIs that matter (ignore the rest):** email waitlist count (the only number that predicts revenue — target 1,500–3,000 by drop day), saves + shares per post (format signal), profile-visit→follow rate. Follower count is a vanity proxy; a 5k account with a 2k waitlist beats a 50k account with none.

## Drop mechanics

1. **Waitlist → early access → public.** 24–48h early-access window for email/SMS list, then public. Sell through the first run; **do not restock fast.** "Sold out, next run in March" builds the brand; instant restock kills urgency.
2. **No discounting. Ever.** Not launch codes, not influencer codes, not Black Friday percent-offs (do a gift-with-purchase or early access instead). Premium muted brands die by markdown — it's the Under Armour lesson and the Vuori counter-lesson. Price honestly, hold the line.
3. **Unit reality check:** 4–5 SKUs × ~100–150 units = a launch you can sell out with a 2k waitlist. Selling out is the story; a warehouse is a liability.
4. **Packaging is content:** black mailer, white CMSN, tagline on the inside flap where they see it on opening. The unboxing is the first repost you'll get from every customer.

## Community language

- Customers/followers: **CMSNers.**
- The walk-to-gym ritual: **The Walk.** (Future hashtag once there's product on bodies: post your walk.)
- The ethic, used in captions and replies constantly: **"Earned, not given"** / "Earn your CMSN." Written, it's always the four letters; spoken, it's the full phrase — the gap between how it reads and how it sounds is an insider signal, and insiders are the point.
- Voice: short sentences, no exclamation marks, gym-literate, Bronx-warm but corporate-clean — the same register as the clothes: mostly quiet, occasionally loud.
